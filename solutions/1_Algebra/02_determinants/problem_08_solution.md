# Exercise 8 - Determinant of a Product

## Question

Given det A = 3 and det B = -5, calculate det(AB), det(BA) and det(A^-1 B). Explain why the first two results are equal.

## Solution

Using the determinant property:

\[
\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)
\]

\[
\det(AB)=3(-5)=-15
\]

Similarly:

\[
\det(BA)=\det(B)\det(A)=(-5)(3)=-15
\]

Since A is invertible:

\[
\det(A^{-1}B)=\det(A^{-1})\det(B)
\]

\[
=\frac{1}{3}(-5)=-\frac{5}{3}
\]

The first two results are equal because multiplication of determinants is commutative, even though matrix multiplication is generally not.
