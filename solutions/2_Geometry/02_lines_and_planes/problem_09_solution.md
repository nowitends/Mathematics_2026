# Exercise 9. Distances

## Given

First, we have a point:

$$
P=(2,3)
$$

And a line:

$$
3x+4y-10=0
$$

Second, we have:

$$
Q=(2,-1,3)
$$

And a plane:

$$
2x-2y+z-4=0
$$

We need to calculate both distances and compare the formulas.

## Solution

### 1. Distance from P to the Line

I use the distance formula:

$$
d=\frac{|ax_0+by_0+c|}
{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}
$$

Substituting the values:

$$
d=\frac{|3(2)+4(3)-10|}
{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}
$$

$$
=\frac{|6+12-10|}{\sqrt{9+16}}
$$

$$
=\frac{8}{5}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{d=\frac85}
$$

### 2. Distance from Q to the Plane

I use the plane distance formula:

$$
d=\frac{|ax_0+by_0+cz_0+d|}
{\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}}
$$

Substituting the values:

$$
d=\frac{|2(2)-2(-1)+3-4|}
{\sqrt{2^2+(-2)^2+1^2}}
$$

$$
=\frac{|4+2+3-4|}{\sqrt{4+4+1}}
$$

$$
=\frac5{3}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{d=\frac53}
$$

### 3. Comparing the Two Formulas

Both formulas calculate the shortest distance from a point to a line or a plane.

The main difference is that the line is in 2D and the plane is in 3D.

Both formulas use the normal direction to find the shortest distance.

## Conclusion

The distance from P to the line is:

$$
\boxed{\frac85}
$$

The distance from Q to the plane is:

$$
\boxed{\frac53}
$$

Both calculations use the normal direction.
