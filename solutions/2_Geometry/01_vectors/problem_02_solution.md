# Exercise 2. Distance as the Length of a Vector

## Given Points

We are given two points:

$$
A=(1,2,3)
$$

$$
B=(4,-2,6)
$$

We need to find the vector from A to B, calculate its length, and compare it with the distance formula.

## Solution

### 1. Construct the Vector AB

To find the vector from A to B, I subtract the coordinates of A from B:

$$
\overrightarrow{AB}=B-A
$$

$$
=(4-1,-2-2,6-3)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\overrightarrow{AB}=(3,-4,3)}
$$

### 2. Calculate Its Length

I use the vector length formula:

$$
|\overrightarrow{AB}|=
\sqrt{3^2+(-4)^2+3^2}
$$

$$
=\sqrt{9+16+9}
$$

$$
\boxed{|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{34}}
$$

### 3. Calculate the Distance Between the Points

Now I use the coordinate distance formula:

$$
d(A,B)=
\sqrt{(4-1)^2+(-2-2)^2+(6-3)^2}
$$

$$
=\sqrt{3^2+(-4)^2+3^2}
$$

$$
=\sqrt{9+16+9}
$$

$$
\boxed{d(A,B)=\sqrt{34}}
$$

### 4. Compare the Results

Both calculations give the same result:

$$
|\overrightarrow{AB}|=d(A,B)=\sqrt{34}
$$

This happens because the distance between two points is the length of the vector connecting them.

## Conclusion

The vector from A to B is:

$$
\boxed{(3,-4,3)}
$$

The distance between the points is:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{34}}
$$

Both methods give the same answer because they use the same coordinate differences.
