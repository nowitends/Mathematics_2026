# Exercise 1. Vector Operations and Their Meaning

## Given Vectors

We are given:

$$
u=(2,-1,3)
$$

$$
v=(-1,4,2)
$$

We need to calculate $u+v$, $u-v$, and $3u-2v$. We also need to verify that $(u+v)-v=u$ and explain vector addition.

## Solution

### 1. Addition of Vectors

I add the corresponding components:

$$
u+v=(2+(-1),-1+4,3+2)
$$

$$
\boxed{u+v=(1,3,5)}
$$

### 2. Subtraction of Vectors

I subtract each component:

$$
u-v=(2-(-1),-1-4,3-2)
$$

$$
\boxed{u-v=(3,-5,1)}
$$

### 3. Calculate 3u - 2v

First, I multiply both vectors by their numbers:

$$
3u=(6,-3,9)
$$

$$
2v=(-2,8,4)
$$

Now I subtract:

$$
3u-2v=(6-(-2),-3-8,9-4)
$$

$$
\boxed{3u-2v=(8,-11,5)}
$$

### 4. Verify the Identity

We need to check:

$$
(u+v)-v=u
$$

Using the result from the first calculation:

$$
(1,3,5)-(-1,4,2)
$$

$$
=(1+1,3-4,5-2)
$$

$$
=(2,-1,3)
$$

This is equal to the original vector $u$.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{(u+v)-v=u}
$$

### Geometric Explanation

Adding two vectors means combining their directions and lengths.

Geometrically, I can place the starting point of the second vector at the endpoint of the first vector. The result is the vector from the beginning of the first one to the end of the second one.

## Conclusion

The results are:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
u+v&=(1,3,5)\\
u-v&=(3,-5,1)\\
3u-2v&=(8,-11,5)
\end{aligned}
}
$$

The identity is also correct because subtracting $v$ from $u+v$ gives the original vector $u$.
